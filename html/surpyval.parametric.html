Parametric

Lifetime distributions with a fixed functional form, fitted by estimating a few parameters. Each distribution is exported as a ready-made instance (surpyval.Weibull, surpyval.LogNormal, …). Its fit accepts any mix of observed, censored and truncated data and returns a Parametric model; its from_params builds the same model from known parameters. The distribution’s own functions (sf, ff, df, hf, Hf, qf, mean, moment, random, …) can also be called directly with the parameters as extra arguments, for example Weibull.sf(x, alpha, beta).

fit supports five estimation methods (how='MLE', 'MPP', 'MOM', 'MSE', 'MPS'), fixed parameters (fixed), an offset (offset=True), a limited failure population (lfp=True) and zero inflation (zi=True); fit_best() fits every candidate distribution and keeps the best (see Comparison Tests and Validation Metrics). The theory is in Parametric Estimation and worked examples are in Parametric SurPyval Modelling.

Parametric Class

The fitted model every distribution’s fit and from_params returns.

  • Parametric Model

Joint Likelihood Contours

The joint likelihood-ratio confidence region of a fit’s two parameters, and a plot of one or more of them.

Discrete Distribution Classes

Discrete lifetimes on the positive integers, for cycle- or demand-counted data. All accept the same censoring and truncation formats as the continuous distributions.

  • Geometric Distribution
  • Poisson Distribution
  • Binomial Distribution
  • Negative Binomial Distribution
  • Beta-Geometric Distribution
  • Discrete Weibull Distribution
  • Discretized Continuous Distributions

Special Distributions

Per-demand and degenerate models with no (or a fixed) time dimension, for composing into mixtures, competing risks and demand studies.

  • Bernoulli Distribution
  • Fixed Event Probability
  • Exact Event Time
  • Degenerate Distributions

Flexible Parametric (Royston-Parmar)

A spline model for data whose hazard no standard distribution fits.

  • Royston-Parmar Flexible Parametric Model

Custom Distributions

Define a new distribution from its cumulative hazard function alone.

  • Custom Distributions

Zero-Failure Analysis (Weibayes)

The bound on a Weibull scale of known shape when there are too few failures to fit one, including none.

  • Weibayes

Mixture Modelling

A population made of several sub-populations, each with its own distribution of the same family.

  • Mixture Models

Equivalent Time Scales

One life measured on a main clock (hours, say) and a subordinate second clock (starts) read at the same moments, combined into one age x + k x2 with k learned from the data. The data are univariate: x, c, n, t on the main clock, plus x2 (an array or a SecondaryClock). See Equivalent Time Scales for the guide.

  • Equivalent Time Scales
