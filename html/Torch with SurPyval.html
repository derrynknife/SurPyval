Torch with SurPyval

If you already train neural networks in PyTorch, surpyval.torch gives your network SurPyval’s likelihood for lifetime data, and gives you SurPyval’s tools for what it predicts. You keep your own network, training loop, optimiser and device; SurPyval supplies the loss and the glue at both ends.

In plain words: a censored or truncated lifetime cannot go into an ordinary regression loss. A unit still running at 3,000 hours tells you only that its life is longer than 3,000 hours; a unit found failed at an inspection, only that it failed between two inspections; a unit you only heard of because it survived to 1,000 hours, that it comes from a population cut off at 1,000 hours. Each needs its own term in the likelihood. surpyval.torch writes those terms as a differentiable torch loss, exactly as SurPyval’s fit counts them, for one distribution per row or a mixture of several (the model of Deep Survival Machines, Nagpal, Li and Dubrawski, 2021).

SurPyval does not install PyTorch and does not depend on it, not even as an optional extra. import surpyval and import surpyval.torch never import it; the helpers import it when you call them, and if it is not installed they stop with a message saying to install it (pip install torch, or the build for your machine from pytorch.org).

The helpers are:

  • targets(): lifetime data (failures, right, left and interval censoring, left and right truncation, counts), checked as fit checks it, as the tensors of a Targets, one row per unit in the order given;

  • constrain(): a network’s raw outputs as a distribution’s parameters, each inside its range;

  • nll(): the exact negative log-likelihood, of one distribution or a mixture (mixture=), and NLLLoss, the same as a torch.nn.Module;

  • sf(): each row’s survival curve as an (N, T) tensor, what score_predictions() scores;

  • to_surpyval(): any row as an ordinary SurPyval model.

They support the Weibull, Exponential, LogNormal, Gamma, LogLogistic, Normal, Gumbel, Logistic and Rayleigh distributions (surpyval.torch.SUPPORTED, the same as the PyMC helpers’); any other is refused with that list.

What SurPyval does not do

There are no training loops, network architectures, hyperparameter searches, early stopping or GPU management here: those are yours, and torch already has them. Nothing in the loss keeps a mixture’s component from collapsing (its weight going to 0, or its scale to a point): mixtures train slowly and can do so, and how to train them (the learning rate, the number of components, a prior or penalty of your own, where to start) is your choice. Train in double precision (.double()) when you want the loss to equal fit’s likelihood: it does to 1e-10 there. Single precision works, and is not held to that: a window far in a tail can round to probability 0 in it, an infinite loss.

A mixture network, trained and scored

Two thousand units with two covariates fail in one of two ways: an early mode, more common the larger the first covariate, whose scale also depends on the second, and a wear-out mode. Each is censored at a random time. A small network gives each unit a mixture of three Weibulls (one more than the truth, as in practice you do not know how many), trained on 1,500 units with Adam, and scored on the other 500.

import numpy as np
import torch
import surpyval as sp
import surpyval.torch as spt
from surpyval.metrics import concordance_index, score_predictions

# Simulated data: two failure modes, whose mix depends on the first
# covariate, and an early mode whose scale depends on the second.
rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
n = 2000
X = rng.normal(size=(n, 2))
early = rng.uniform(size=n) < 1 / (1 + np.exp(-1.5 * X[:, 0]))
life = np.where(
    early,
    np.exp(1.8 + 0.4 * X[:, 1]) * rng.weibull(1.5, n),
    40.0 * rng.weibull(3.0, n),
)
censor = rng.uniform(10, 80, n)
x, c = np.minimum(life, censor), (life > censor).astype(int)
train, test = slice(0, 1500), slice(1500, None)

# Your network: K = 3 weights and K Weibulls' two parameters per row.
K = 3
torch.manual_seed(0)
net = torch.nn.Sequential(
    torch.nn.Linear(2, 32), torch.nn.ReLU(), torch.nn.Linear(32, K * 3)
).double()
# Start the components apart: scales at the quartiles of the times,
# shapes at 1.5 (the bias is the inverse of constrain's softplus).
start = [np.quantile(x[train], q) for q in (0.25, 0.5, 0.75)]
with torch.no_grad():
    net[2].bias[K:] = torch.tensor(
        np.log(np.expm1([v for a in start for v in (a, 1.5)]))
    )

def predict(features):
    out = net(torch.as_tensor(features))
    logits, raw = out[:, :K], out[:, K:].reshape(-1, K, 2)
    return spt.constrain(sp.Weibull, raw), logits

y = spt.targets(x=x[train], c=c[train])
loss_fn = spt.NLLLoss(sp.Weibull)
optimiser = torch.optim.Adam(net.parameters(), lr=0.01)
for epoch in range(1000):
    optimiser.zero_grad()
    params, logits = predict(X[train])
    loss = loss_fn(y, params, mixture=logits)
    loss.backward()
    optimiser.step()
print(f"loss per unit: {loss.item():.4f}")

# Score the held-out rows with SurPyval's metrics.
times = [5.0, 10.0, 20.0, 40.0]
with torch.no_grad():
    params, logits = predict(X[test])
    S = spt.sf(sp.Weibull, times, params, mixture=logits).numpy()
scores = score_predictions(x[test], c[test], S, times)
print(scores.scores.round(3).to_string(index=False))
print(concordance_index(x[test], c[test], 1 - S[:, 2]).summary())

# Any row as an ordinary SurPyval model.
unit = spt.to_surpyval(sp.Weibull, params, row=0, mixture=logits)
print(unit.w.round(3), unit.params.round(2).tolist())
print(unit.sf(times).round(4), S[0].round(4))

The first held-out unit’s mixture puts 0.78 of its weight on a wear-out mode (scale 40, shape 2.8; the truth’s are 40 and 3) and 0.16 on an early one (scale 4.9, shape 2.7); the third component is small, and its survival function as a SurPyval model is the row of S. The start matters: from the network’s random bias instead of the quartiles, the same loop ended at 3.12 per unit, with two of that unit’s three components collapsed (weights below 1e-6, shapes near 0.03). That is for the training to deal with, not the loss.

What each step does:

  • The network ends in K * 3 outputs per unit: K logits, whose softmax is the mixture’s weights, and two raw values for each of the K Weibulls, reshaped to (N, K, 2). constrain() maps those into each parameter’s range (a softplus for a positive one), giving alpha and beta of shape (N, K).

  • NLLLoss(sp.Weibull)(y, params, mixture=logits) is the negative log-likelihood per unit (reduction="mean", divided by the total count). Each unit’s term is the log of the weighted sum of the components’ likelihoods of what was seen of it: a density for a failure, a survival probability for a unit still running, and the mixture’s probability of a window for an interval. A truncation window divides by the mixture’s probability of it.

  • sf() gives the held-out units’ survival curves at the scoring times, an (N, T) array once detached: score_predictions() gives the Brier score at each time, the integrated Brier score and the time-dependent AUC, each with its standard error and interval, and concordance_index() Harrell’s C of the risk 1 - S(t) at a horizon, with its interval.

  • to_surpyval() turns the first held-out unit’s prediction into a MixtureModel (for a single distribution, dist.from_params(...)): its sf, ff, hf, qf, mean and random are SurPyval’s, and its survival function equals that row of sf.

To draw one unit’s curve:

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

t = np.linspace(0, 80, 200)
plt.plot(t, unit.sf(t))
plt.xlabel("time")
plt.ylabel("probability of surviving")

Data and batches

targets takes the data in any form fit does: x, c, n, t or tl/tr, xl/xr, or a SurpyvalData (whose rows are its own, grouped and sorted unless built with group_and_sort=False). Row i of the result is row i of your features. A Targets is a named tuple, so it batches as a tuple does:

loader = torch.utils.data.DataLoader(
    torch.utils.data.TensorDataset(torch.as_tensor(X), *y),
    batch_size=256, shuffle=True,
)
for features, *fields in loader:
    params, logits = predict(features)
    loss = loss_fn(spt.Targets(*fields), params, mixture=logits)

A parameter can be one value per row ((N,), or (N, K) in a mixture), one value shared by every row (a scalar, or (K,) per component in a mixture), or a mix: a shared shape and a scale per unit is {"alpha": scale_per_row, "beta": shape_parameter}. The loss’s type and device are those of the parameters.

One distribution per row is the same without mixture=: raw of shape (N, k), constrain gives (N,) parameters, and sf and to_surpyval take no logits. A mixture of one component is exactly that.

The loss

Each row adds one term, times its count n: log f(x) for a failure, log S(x) right censored, log F(x) left censored and log[F(xr) - F(xl)] for an interval; a row observed only inside the window (tl, tr] subtracts log[F(tr) - F(tl)]. For a mixture, each is the log of the weighted sum of the components’ (worked out in log space), so a window is the mixture’s F(b) - F(a). Every term is written in a form that is stable in both tails, and each row is given only the formula of its own kind: the loss and its gradient are finite wherever the likelihood is, including where one component of a mixture gives a row probability 0 and another explains it. The tests hold the loss to SurPyval’s own likelihood (fit’s, and MixtureModel’s) to 1e-10 in double precision, for every family, kind of row and truncation.

The gradient is first-order; for the Gamma distribution, whose derivative in its shape is numerical, it cannot be differentiated again. Because each kind of row is gathered on its own, the loss is for eager torch, not torch.vmap or tracing.

  • c (array-like, optional) – The data as arrays in SurPyval’s usual forms (see Conventions).

  • xr (array-like, optional) – The data as arrays in SurPyval’s usual forms (see Conventions).

  • tl (array-like, optional) – The data as arrays in SurPyval’s usual forms (see Conventions).

  • tr (array-like, optional) – The data as arrays in SurPyval’s usual forms (see Conventions).

  • dtype (torch.dtype, optional) – The floating type of the tensors; torch.float64 if not given. nll() converts the target to its parameters’ type, so this only matters for what you store.

surpyval.torch.constrain(dist: Any, raw: Any) → dict

Unconstrained values, such as a network’s outputs, as the parameters of dist, each inside its range.

Column j of raw becomes the j-th parameter of dist (dist.parameter_names), through the map its range (dist.bounds) calls for: softplus for a positive parameter, the identity for an unbounded one, and a scaled sigmoid for one between two finite bounds. Every map is smooth and increasing, so a gradient reaches the network through it.

Parameters:

  • dist (distribution) – One of SUPPORTED, such as surpyval.Weibull.

  • raw (Tensor) – Shape (N, k) for N rows, or (k,) for one set of parameters shared by every row, where k is the number of parameters of dist; (N, K, k) (or (K, k)) for the K components of a mixture.

Returns:

Each parameter’s tensor by name, of shape (N,) (or a scalar tensor from a (k,) input; (N, K) or (K,) for a mixture): what nll() takes.

Return type:

dict

Notes

softplus of a very negative value rounds to 0, which is not inside a positive range: a value that rounds onto a bound is kept one step of its type inside it. For a network that should start at a sensible model, set its last layer’s bias to the inverse map of a fit’s estimates (log(expm1(value)) for a positive parameter).

Examples

>>> import pytest; torch = pytest.importorskip("torch")
>>> import surpyval as sp
>>> import surpyval.torch as spt
>>> raw = torch.tensor([[0.0, 1.0], [-2.0, 3.0]], dtype=torch.float64)
>>> params = spt.constrain(sp.Weibull, raw)
>>> list(params)
['alpha', 'beta']
>>> [round(v, 6) for v in params["alpha"].tolist()]
[0.693147, 0.126928]
>>> list(spt.constrain(sp.LogNormal, raw)["mu"].tolist())
[0.0, -2.0]
surpyval.torch.nll(dist: Any, target: Any, params: Any, *, mixture: Any = None, reduction: str = 'mean') → Any

The negative log-likelihood of censored and truncated data as a torch loss: the likelihood fit maximises, differentiable in the parameters.

Each row contributes its own term, times its count n: log f(x) for a failure (c = 0), log S(x) right censored (c = 1), log F(x) left censored (c = -1) and log[F(xr) - F(xl)] interval censored (c = 2); a row observed only inside the window (tl, tr] subtracts log[F(tr) - F(tl)]. A censored row with a finite truncation bound on its censored side is an interval ending at that bound, as in fit. In double precision the sum equals the distribution’s own log-likelihood to 1e-10.

Parameters:

  • dist (distribution) – One of SUPPORTED, such as surpyval.Weibull.

  • target (Targets) – The data, from targets() (or a batch of its rows: the six tensors indexed alike).

  • params (dict) – Every parameter of dist by name, as constrain() returns: each a scalar (a tensor or a number), shared by every row, or a tensor of one value per row, in the target’s row order. Each must be inside its range. With mixture, the components’ parameters: each of shape (N, K), one value per row and component, (K,) for one value per component shared by every row, or a scalar shared by all.

  • mixture (Tensor, optional) – For a mixture of K components of dist (the model of Deep Survival Machines): the logits of the components’ weights, shape (N, K), or (K,) for weights shared by every row; the weights are their softmax over the last axis. Each row’s term is then the log of the weighted sum of the components’ likelihoods of the row, less the log of the weighted sum of their probabilities of its truncation window: an interval’s probability is the mixture’s F(xr) - F(xl). With K = 1 the loss is that without a mixture.

  • reduction ({"mean", "sum", "none"}, default "mean") – "sum" is the negative log-likelihood; "mean" divides it by the total count sum(n), the loss per unit, which does not change with the batch size; "none" gives each row’s term (times its count), of shape (N,).

Returns:

The loss, of the parameters’ floating type and on their device (the target is converted to both). With no tensor among the parameters it is torch.float64.

Return type:

Tensor

Notes

The value is finite, with a finite gradient, wherever the likelihood is: each row is given only the formula of its own kind, in a form that is stable in both tails. Double precision is what the 1e-10 agreement with fit holds for; single precision works and is not held to it (a window far in a tail can round to probability 0 there, an infinite loss). The gradient is of the first order: the Gamma distribution’s derivative in its shape cannot be differentiated again.

In a mixture, a component can give a row probability 0 in double precision (the row is far in its tail) while another explains it: the loss and its gradient stay finite, that component taking no part in the row’s gradient. Nothing in the loss keeps a component from collapsing (a weight going to 0, or a scale to a point): mixtures train slowly and can do so, and how to train them is yours to choose.

Examples

>>> import pytest; torch = pytest.importorskip("torch")
>>> import surpyval as sp
>>> import surpyval.torch as spt
>>> y = spt.targets(x=[1, 3, [2, 4]], c=[0, 1, 2])
>>> params = {"alpha": torch.tensor(3.0, dtype=torch.float64),
...           "beta": torch.tensor(1.5, dtype=torch.float64)}
>>> round(float(spt.nll(sp.Weibull, y, params, reduction="sum")), 6)
3.440673
>>> data = sp.SurpyvalData(x=[1, 3, [2, 4]], c=[0, 1, 2])
>>> ll = sp.Weibull._log_likelihood(data, 3.0, 1.5, 0, 0, 1)
>>> round(float(-ll), 6)
3.440673

A network’s raw outputs become parameters through constrain():

>>> raw = torch.zeros(3, 2, dtype=torch.float64, requires_grad=True)
>>> loss = spt.nll(sp.Weibull, y, spt.constrain(sp.Weibull, raw))
>>> loss.backward()
>>> raw.grad.shape

surpyval.torch.sf(dist: Any, times: Any, params: Any, *, mixture: Any = None) → Any

The survival function of each row at each of times, as a tensor: the predictions score_predictions takes.

Parameters:

  • dist (distribution) – One of SUPPORTED, such as surpyval.Weibull.

  • times (array-like or Tensor) – The T times to evaluate at, shape (T,) (or one number). Every row is evaluated at all of them.

  • params (dict) – Every parameter of dist by name, as constrain() returns and nll() takes: each a scalar or one value per row (with mixture, (N, K), (K,) or a scalar).

  • mixture (Tensor, optional) – The logits of a mixture’s weights, (N, K) or (K,), as nll() takes them: each row’s survival function is then sum_k w_k S_k(t).

Returns:

Shape (N, T), one row per row of the parameters (N = 1 where every parameter is shared), of the parameters’ floating type and on their device. It is differentiable in the parameters (and the logits); for score_predictions and other NumPy code use .detach().cpu().numpy().

Return type:

Tensor

Notes

A time at or below the start of the distribution’s support has survival 1, and one at or past its end 0, as for SurPyval’s models. Row i equals to_surpyval(dist, params, row=i).sf(times).

Examples

>>> import pytest; torch = pytest.importorskip("torch")
>>> import surpyval as sp
>>> import surpyval.torch as spt
>>> params = {"alpha": torch.tensor([10.0, 20.0], dtype=torch.float64),
...           "beta": torch.tensor(2.0, dtype=torch.float64)}
>>> spt.sf(sp.Weibull, [0.0, 10.0, 20.0], params).numpy().round(4)
array([[1.    , 0.3679, 0.0183],
       [1.    , 0.7788, 0.3679]])

A mixture’s, row by row:

>>> params = spt.constrain(sp.Weibull, torch.zeros(2, 3, 2))
>>> spt.sf(sp.Weibull, [1.0, 2.0], params,
...        mixture=torch.zeros(2, 3)).shape
torch.Size([2, 2])
surpyval.torch.to_surpyval(dist: Any, params: Any, *, row: Any = None, mixture: Any = None) → Any

One row’s prediction as an ordinary SurPyval model: its sf, ff, df, hf, qf, mean, random and plots.

Parameters:

  • dist (distribution) – One of SUPPORTED, such as surpyval.Weibull.

  • params (dict) – Every parameter of dist by name, as nll() takes them (with mixture, the components’).

  • row (int, optional) – The row to turn into a model, from 0 to N - 1 (negative counts from the end). It may be left out only where the parameters have one row (each is shared).

  • mixture (Tensor, optional) – The logits of a mixture’s weights, as nll() takes them.

Returns:

dist.from_params(...) of the row’s parameters, or, with mixture, MixtureModel.from_params(dist, ...) of its components’ parameters and weights. The values are taken out of the graph as double-precision numbers; the model knows nothing of torch.

Return type:

Parametric or MixtureModel

>>> model.params, round(float(model.sf(20.0)), 4)
(array([20.,  2.]), 0.3679)

A mixture’s row is a MixtureModel:

>>> params = {"alpha": torch.tensor([10.0, 50.0], dtype=torch.float64),
...           "beta": torch.tensor([2.0, 4.0], dtype=torch.float64)}
>>> logits = torch.log(torch.tensor([0.3, 0.7], dtype=torch.float64))
>>> mm = spt.to_surpyval(sp.Weibull, params, mixture=logits)
>>> mm.w.round(6), mm.sf([5, 40]).round(4)
(array([0.3, 0.7]), array([0.9336, 0.4647]))

NLLLoss(dist, reduction="mean") is nll() as a torch.nn.Module: NLLLoss(dist)(target, params, mixture=None) is nll(dist, target, params, mixture=mixture). It is built when first used, so that import surpyval.torch does not import torch.
