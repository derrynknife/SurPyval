Competing Risks

Models for units that can fail from one of several distinct causes, where the occurrence of one cause removes the unit from risk of the others. Import them from surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.

For a narrative introduction with worked examples, see Competing Risks SurPyval Modelling; for the statistical background, see Competing Risks Analysis. The Gray test for comparing cumulative incidence between groups is documented with the other hypothesis tests in Comparison Tests and Validation Metrics.

Non-Parametric (Aalen-Johansen)

The cumulative incidence of each cause estimated without a model, the incidence-increment helper it (and Gray’s test) is built on, and the variance its confidence bounds (cb) use.

class surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.nonparametric.competing_risks.CompetingRisks

Bases: SerialisableMixin

Non-parametric competing-risks estimate: the Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence function (CIF) of each cause, and the all-cause and cause-specific (net) hazards and survival.

Each unit fails from one of several causes e (or is right-censored, with no cause). The cumulative incidence of cause \(j\) is the probability of failing from that cause by time \(t\) while the others still act,

\[F_j(t) = \sum_{t_i \le t} S(t_{i-1}) \frac{d_{ij}}{r_i},\]

with \(S\) the all-cause Kaplan-Meier survival, \(d_{ij}\) the failures from cause \(j\) at \(t_i\) and \(r_i\) the number at risk. The CIFs of all causes add up to the all-cause failure probability.

With delayed entry (left truncation, tl) a unit is at risk only between its entry and its exit, \(tl < t \le x\), so \(r_i\) counts the units that have entered before \(t_i\) and not yet left. The estimates are then conditional on surviving to the earliest entry, as those of KaplanMeier with tl are.

Failures found at inspections (left-censored, c = -1, or interval-censored, c = 2 or a window [xl, xr]) have no exact time, so for data with any such row fit gives instead the non-parametric maximum-likelihood estimate (NPMLE) of the cumulative incidences (Hudgens, Satten and Longini 2001), the competing-risks form of Turnbull’s estimate; it also takes failures whose cause was not recorded (masked="include"). Data with only exact and right-censored rows keep the Aalen-Johansen estimate. estimator says which was used ("Aalen-Johansen" or "NPMLE"); see fit() for what each offers.

Call the class method CompetingRisks.fit (or fit_from_df); it returns a fitted instance.

References

Hudgens, M. G., Satten, G. A. and Longini, I. M. (2001), “Nonparametric maximum likelihood estimation for competing risks survival data subject to interval censoring and truncation”, Biometrics 57, 74-80.

Hf(x: ArrayLike, event: Any = None) → NDArray

Cumulative hazard, all causes (event=None) or one cause. With the Nelson-Aalen method it is the sum of the hazard increments d / r; with Kaplan-Meier it is -log of the product-limit survival, so that sf == exp(-Hf) either way.

cb(x: ArrayLike, event: Any = None, on: str = 'cif', alpha_ci: float = 0.05, bound: str = 'two-sided', bound_type: str = 'exp', n_boot: int | None = None, random_state: Any = None) → NDArray

Pointwise confidence bounds on the cumulative incidence of cause event (on="cif", the default), or on the survival, failure probability or cumulative hazard (on="sf", "ff", "Hf") of all causes (event=None) or one cause’s net ones.

The cumulative incidence’s variance is Aalen’s (1978) estimate, the one R’s cmprsk::cuminc reports as var (see aalen_johansen_variance()). It is the same whichever how the model was fitted with, as the cumulative incidence is. The bounds on sf, ff and Hf are those of the single-event estimate of the same data (the all-cause failures, or the cause’s with the other causes censored), by the fitted how: KaplanMeier’s cb (Greenwood’s variance) or NelsonAalen’s (Aalen’s).

For a model fitted with weights w every variance is the robust (infinitesimal jackknife) one instead: the cumulative incidence’s is that of R’s survfit(Surv(x, factor(e)) ~ 1, weights = w)$std.err (see weighted_aalen_johansen()), and the bounds on sf, ff and Hf are those of the weighted KaplanMeier or NelsonAalen fit of the same data.

The bounds hold at each x separately.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like) – The times at which to bound the function.

  • event (optional) – The cause; required for on="cif". For sf, ff and Hf, None (the default) is all causes and a cause its net function (the other causes treated as censoring).

  • on (('cif', 'sf', 'ff', 'Hf'), str, optional) – The function to bound (‘R’ and ‘F’ are accepted for ‘sf’ and ‘ff’). Defaults to ‘cif’.

  • alpha_ci (float, optional) – The significance level: 0.05 (the default) gives a 95% interval.

  • bound (('two-sided', 'lower', 'upper'), str, optional) – The two-sided interval (the default), with one row per x and the columns [lower, upper], or one side. A one-sided bound puts all of alpha_ci on one side, so it equals that end of the two-sided interval at 2 * alpha_ci.

  • bound_type (('exp', 'normal'), str, optional) – ‘exp’ (the default) forms the interval on the log(-log) scale, \(\hat{F}^{\exp(\pm z\,\hat{\sigma} / (\hat{F}\log \hat{F}))}\) for the cumulative incidence (Choudhury, 2002), which keeps it within [0, 1]; ‘normal’ is \(\hat{F} \pm z\,\hat{\sigma}\), which can leave it. For sf, ff and Hf it is the single-event cb’s bound_type, which also takes ‘log’, ‘logit’, ‘arcsine’, ‘peto’ and ‘rothman’ (see KaplanMeier.cb).

  • n_boot (optional) – The bootstrap samples and seed of the bounds of a model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows (estimator == "NPMLE", whose bounds are the case bootstrap’s); the Aalen-Johansen bounds above are not resampled and refuse them.

  • random_state (optional) – The bootstrap samples and seed of the bounds of a model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows (estimator == "NPMLE", whose bounds are the case bootstrap’s); the Aalen-Johansen bounds above are not resampled and refuse them.

Returns:

The bounds at each x: [lower, upper] rows for a two-sided bound, one value per x for one side.

Return type:

numpy array

Raises:

ValueError – If on, bound or bound_type is not one of the values above, alpha_ci is not between 0 and 1, event is not a cause of the model, or on="cif" without an event.

Notes

Where the variance is zero the bounds are the estimate: before the cause’s first event the cumulative incidence is exactly 0, and so are both bounds; before the first observed time they are the estimate’s initial value (0, or 1 on sf). Above the last observed time they are NaN, with a warning naming the times (the estimate only holds its last value there), unless the model has a support (see set_support()): then the bounds at the last time are carried to upper, and they are NaN outside [lower, upper]. If a cause’s cumulative incidence reaches 1 (every failure from that cause and no one left at risk) the ‘exp’ upper bound there is 1 and the lower bound the largest one before it.

There are no bounds on hf, df or iif, by design and as for the single-event estimates (KaplanMeier.cb refuses ‘hf’ and ‘df’ too): they are the jumps of the step estimate, point masses at the event times and 0 between them, so a pointwise interval would be one about a single time’s increment and nothing anywhere else. Bound the cumulative functions instead.

References

Aalen, O. (1978), “Nonparametric estimation of partial transition probabilities in multiple decrement models”, The Annals of Statistics, 6(3), 534-545.

Choudhury, J. B. (2002), “Non-parametric confidence interval estimation for competing risks analysis: application to contraceptive data”, Statistics in Medicine, 21(8), 1129-1144.

ff(x: ArrayLike, event: Any = None) → NDArray

1 - sf: all causes, or the net failure probability from one cause with the others removed (treated as censoring). For the probability of failing from a cause while the others still act, use cif().

The all-cause ff is by the estimator the model was fitted with (how). The cumulative incidences always use the Kaplan-Meier survival, so they add up to ff only for a model fitted with how="Kaplan-Meier"; with the default, Nelson-Aalen, ff = 1 - exp(-H) is below their sum from the first event on.

classmethod fit(x: ArrayLike | None = None, e: ArrayLike | None = None, c: ArrayLike | None = None, n: ArrayLike | None = None, how: str = 'Nelson-Aalen', w: ArrayLike | None = None, tl: ArrayLike | float | None = None, xl: ArrayLike | None = None, xr: ArrayLike | None = None, masked: str = 'refuse', tol: float = 1e-09, max_iter: int = 10000, *, unit=None) → CompetingRisks

Fit the non-parametric competing-risks model.

Data with only exact and right-censored rows get the Aalen-Johansen estimate. Data with any left-censored (c = -1) or interval-censored (c = 2, a window [xl, xr]) row, or a failure whose cause was not recorded taken with masked="include", get the non-parametric maximum-likelihood estimate (NPMLE) of Hudgens, Satten and Longini (2001), the competing-risks form of Turnbull’s estimate; on exact data the two agree (with how="Kaplan-Meier"). The model’s estimator says which was used.

The NPMLE puts each cause’s probability mass on the innermost intervals of the windows that can hold it and chooses the masses that make the data most likely (the EM algorithm, accelerated by SQUAREM, stopped when the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions hold to tol; log_likelihood, converged, gap and iterations report the search). Inside an interval the cumulative incidence is held and rises at the interval’s end, as Turnbull’s curve does; innermost_intervals(event) gives the intervals and masses, and beyond the mass of the units not failed by the end of follow-up. It offers cif, the all-cause sf / ff / Hf, cb (the case bootstrap: n_boot, random_state), plot and to_dict; what needs each failure’s time is refused, saying what to use instead: a cause’s net survival (sf(x, event)), hf, df, iif, rmtl, set_support, the stacked plot and the analytic bounds’ bound_type. Weights and delayed entry are not taken with such rows.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like) – Failure or censoring times: one column, with the flags c, or two columns, each row the window [xl, xr] the failure lies in (as every SurPyval fit takes it). Leave it out to give xl and xr.

  • e (array_like) – The cause of each failure: any hashable labels (integers, strings, tuples, or a mix); they are sorted to fix their order in event_idx_map (labels of different types by type name, then text). A missing value (None, NaN) marks a right-censored row.

  • c (array_like, optional) – Censoring flags: 0 a failure (with a cause in e), 1 right-censored (with e missing), -1 left censored (found failed at x, some time before), 2 interval censored (failed in (xl, xr]). If omitted, a row with no cause and one time is right censored and every other row’s flag comes from its times.

  • n (array_like, optional) – Counts. Defaults to 1.

  • how (str, optional) – The all-cause survival estimator that sf, ff and Hf report: "Nelson-Aalen" (the default, exp(-H)) or "Kaplan-Meier". The cumulative incidence always uses the Kaplan-Meier survival, so the CIFs add up to the Kaplan-Meier failure probability either way: to the model’s ff with "Kaplan-Meier", but not with the default (see ff()).

  • w (array_like, optional) – Sampling or inverse-probability weights, one positive number per row (or one for every row): each unit stands for w units of the population. The numbers at risk and of events count n * w, and every variance is the robust (infinitesimal jackknife) one, as R’s survfit(Surv(x, factor(e)) ~ 1, weights = w) computes it; w = 1 gives the unweighted estimates with that robust variance, not Aalen’s. See Weighted Data.

  • tl (array_like or scalar, optional) – Delayed entry (left truncation): each unit’s entry time, or one number for every unit. A unit is at risk only in (tl, x], so each tl must be below its x; a unit that failed before it would have entered is never seen. As R’s survfit(Surv(tl, x, factor(e)) ~ 1) the estimates are then conditional on surviving to the earliest entry. Right truncation is not supported (fit(data) refuses data that has it). Not taken with left- or interval-censored rows.

  • xl (array_like, optional) – In place of x: each row’s lower and upper time (equal for an exact time, xr infinite for a right-censored row).

  • xr (array_like, optional) – In place of x: each row’s lower and upper time (equal for an exact time, xr infinite for a right-censored row).

  • masked (str, optional) – A failure with no cause in e: "refuse" (the default) refuses it, as every competing-risks fit does; "include" counts it as a failure from some cause in its window (its likelihood is the sum over the causes), which needs the NPMLE. This assumes that whether a cause was recorded does not depend on which cause it was.

  • tol (float, optional) – The NPMLE’s convergence tolerance: the largest relative gap in the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions. Defaults to 1e-9.

  • max_iter (int, optional) – The NPMLE’s most accelerated EM iterations. Defaults to 10000.

Returns:

The fitted model.

Return type:

CompetingRisks

plot(stacked: bool = False, ax: Any = None, plot_bounds: bool = True, alpha_ci: float = 0.05, bound: str = 'two-sided', bound_type: str = 'exp', n_boot: int | None = None, random_state: Any = None) → Any

Plot the cumulative incidence of every cause (#485), with its confidence bounds.

By default each cause’s cumulative incidence is its own step curve in the theme’s next colour (surpyval.plotting), with its two-sided bounds, its cb() (on="cif"), as a pale band of that colour (a one-sided bound as a dashed step line), unless plot_bounds=False. stacked=True stacks them instead, the causes’ layers separated by a thin white edge, so that the top of the stack is the all-cause failure probability \(1 - S\). A cause’s bounds do not bound its layer (which sits on the causes below it), so stacked, the bounds drawn are the top’s: the Kaplan-Meier (Greenwood, or robust with weights) bounds of the all-cause failures, as KaplanMeier’s plot of the same data draws them (cb(x, on="ff") of a model fitted with how="Kaplan-Meier"), as two thin grey step lines.

Changed in version 0.25: Unstacked by default (it stacked), in the package’s theme.

  • plot_bounds (bool, optional) – Whether to draw the confidence bounds. Defaults to True.

  • alpha_ci (optional) – Passed to the confidence bound calculation; see cb().

  • bound (optional) – Passed to the confidence bound calculation; see cb().

  • bound_type (optional) – Passed to the confidence bound calculation; see cb().

  • n_boot (optional) – For a model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows only (its bootstrap bounds; see cb()).

  • random_state (optional) – For a model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows only (its bootstrap bounds; see cb()).

rmtl(event: Any, tau: float | None = None, alpha_ci: float = 0.05) → dict

Restricted mean time lost to cause event up to tau, with inference.

It is the expected time, within the first tau, that a unit loses to the cause: the area under the cause’s cumulative incidence,

\[\text{RMTL}_k(\tau) = \int_0^\tau F_k(t)\,dt = E[\tau - \min(T, \tau);\ \text{cause } k],\]

exact for the step estimate (Andersen, 2013). The times lost to all the causes add up to tau less the restricted mean survival time; with one cause the time lost is tau - rmst.

The standard error is the delta method’s on the hazard increments (see aalen_johansen_rmtl()): with one cause it is the Greenwood-based standard error of the restricted mean survival time, as KaplanMeier.rmst gives it. The interval is the normal one, rmtl +- z se, as for rmst.

Parameters:

  • event (hashable) – The cause.

  • tau (scalar, optional) – The horizon; defaults to the largest observed time. A tau beyond it is refused while some units are still event-free there (the incidence beyond it is not estimated), as rmst refuses it.

  • alpha_ci (scalar, optional) – Significance level for the interval (default 0.05).

Returns:

{"rmtl", "se", "lower", "upper", "tau"}.

Return type:

dict

Raises:

ValueError – If event is not a cause of the model, tau is negative or past the data (above), or the model was fitted with weights (no standard error is built for those).

References

Andersen, P. K. (2013), “Decomposition of number of life years lost according to causes of death”, Statistics in Medicine, 32(30), 5278-5285.

Zhao, L., Tian, L., Claggett, B., Pfeffer, M., Kim, D. H., Solomon, S. and Wei, L. J. (2018), “Estimating treatment effect with clinical interpretation from a comparative clinical trial with an end point subject to competing risks”, JAMA Cardiology, 3(4), 357-358.

Variance of the Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence of one cause.

Aalen’s (1978) estimate of the asymptotic variance, as R’s cmprsk::cuminc reports it (var; it is the same number). With \(\hat{S}\) the all-cause Kaplan-Meier survival, \(\hat{F}\) the cause’s cumulative incidence and \(\hat{F}_j = \hat{F}(x_j)\),

\[\widehat{Var}\,\hat{F}(t) = \sum_{x_j \le t} \frac{\hat{S}(x_{j-1})^2}{r_j^2}\left[ c(d_{k,j})\, d_{k,j} \left(1 + \frac{\hat{F}_j - \hat{F}(t)}{\hat{S}(x_j)}\right)^2 + c(d_j - d_{k,j})\,(d_j - d_{k,j}) \left(\frac{\hat{F}_j - \hat{F}(t)}{\hat{S}(x_j)}\right)^2 \right],\]

where \(c(m) = (r_j - m) / (r_j - 1)\) for \(m > 1\) tied events (1 otherwise) corrects for ties. Where \(\hat{S}(x_j) = 0\) (the last event, with no one left), cmprsk’s convention is kept: the other causes’ term is left out and the cause’s is \(\hat{S}(x_{j-1})^2 c\, d_{k,j} / r_j^2\).

Parameters:

  • r (array_like) – The number at risk at each distinct time.

  • d (array_like) – The number of events of any cause at each time.

  • d_k (array_like) – The number of events of the cause at each time.

Returns:

The variance of the cause’s cumulative incidence at each time; 0 before its first event.

Return type:

numpy array

References

Compare the restricted mean time lost (RMTL) to a cause between two groups.

The RMTL to a cause up to tau is the expected time, within the first tau, lost to that cause: the area under its cumulative incidence. The difference reads directly as how much more time group a loses to the cause than group b within the horizon, and needs no proportional-hazards assumption; it is the competing-risks counterpart of surpyval.rmst_diff().

Parameters:

  • model_a (CompetingRisks or ParametricCompetingRisks) – Two fitted competing-risks models, one per group, each with the cause event: non-parametric (CompetingRisks.fit per group) or parametric fits, or one of each. Their RMTL and standard error are those of their rmtl.

  • model_b (CompetingRisks or ParametricCompetingRisks) – Two fitted competing-risks models, one per group, each with the cause event: non-parametric (CompetingRisks.fit per group) or parametric fits, or one of each. Their RMTL and standard error are those of their rmtl.

  • event (hashable) – The cause.

  • tau (scalar, optional) – Common horizon. Defaults to the smaller of the two groups’ largest observed times, so both incidences are supported by data up to tau. A non-parametric group refuses a tau past its largest observed time while some units are still event-free there (as surpyval.rmst_diff()); a parametric fit extrapolates.

  • alpha_ci (scalar, optional) – Significance level for the intervals and the test (default 0.05).

Returns:

{"difference", "se", "lower", "upper", "p_value", "ratio", "ratio_lower", "ratio_upper", "rmtl_a", "rmtl_b", "tau"}. difference is RMTL_a - RMTL_b, se the square root of the sum of the groups’ variances (the groups are independent), lower and upper are difference +- z se, and p_value the two-sided z-test of no difference. ratio is RMTL_a / RMTL_b with its interval from the delta method on the log scale, \(\exp(\log \text{ratio} \pm z \sqrt{v_a / \mu_a^2 + v_b / \mu_b^2})\) (NaN where a group’s RMTL is 0).

Return type:

dict

Raises:

ValueError – If event is not a cause of both models, a model cannot give a standard error (see their rmtl), or tau is past a non-parametric group’s largest observed time while it still has units event-free there.

surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.rmtl.aalen_johansen_rmtl(x: ArrayLike, r: ArrayLike, d: ArrayLike, d_k: ArrayLike, tau: float) → tuple[float, float]

The restricted mean time lost to one cause up to tau, from the Aalen-Johansen estimate, and its delta-method variance.

The time lost is the area under the cause’s cumulative incidence, \(\int_0^\tau \hat F_k(t)\,dt\), exact for the step function. Its variance is the delta method’s on the hazard increments at each distinct time, each a multinomial proportion of the \(r_j\) at risk (the cause’s, \(p_{kj} = d_{kj} / r_j\), and the other causes’, \(p_{oj}\)): with \(G_{kj}\) and \(G_{oj}\) the derivatives of the area in them,

\[\widehat{Var} = \sum_{x_j \le \tau} \frac{G_{kj}^2 p_{kj}(1 - p_{kj}) + G_{oj}^2 p_{oj}(1 - p_{oj}) - 2 G_{kj} G_{oj} p_{kj} p_{oj}}{r_j},\]
\[G_{oj} = -\frac{\hat S(x_{j-1})}{\hat S(x_j)}\left(A_j - \hat F_k(x_j) L_j\right), \quad G_{kj} = \hat S(x_{j-1}) L_j + G_{oj},\]

where \(L_j = \tau - x_j\), \(A_j = \int_{x_j}^\tau \hat F_k(t)\,dt\) and \(\hat S\) is the all-cause Kaplan-Meier survival. With one cause it is the Greenwood-based variance of the restricted mean survival time (the time lost is \(\tau\) less it). Where \(\hat S(x_j) = 0\) the incidence is flat from there on, and the ratio is taken as 0.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like) – The distinct times, the numbers at risk and of events (all causes) at each.

  • r (array_like) – The distinct times, the numbers at risk and of events (all causes) at each.

  • d (array_like) – The distinct times, the numbers at risk and of events (all causes) at each.

  • d_k (array_like) – The events of the cause at each time.

  • tau (float) – The horizon.

Returns:

(rmtl, variance).

Return type:

tuple of float

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.rmtl import (
...     aalen_johansen_rmtl,
... )
>>> x, r = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 3.0]), np.array([3.0, 2.0, 1.0])
>>> d, d_a = np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0]), np.array([1.0, 0.0, 1.0])
>>> rmtl, var = aalen_johansen_rmtl(x, r, d, d_a, 4.0)

cb(x: ArrayLike, event: Any = None, on: str = 'cif', alpha_ci: float = 0.05, bound: str = 'two-sided', method: str = 'wald', n_boot: int = 200, random_state: Any = None) → NDArray

Pointwise confidence bounds on the cumulative incidence of cause event (on="cif", the default), or on the survival, failure probability or cumulative hazard (on="sf", "ff", "Hf") of all causes (event=None) or one cause’s net ones, as CompetingRisks.cb bounds them for the non-parametric fit.

The Wald bounds carry the uncertainty of every cause’s parameters (covariance()) to the function by the delta method, on a scale that keeps them in range:

  • the all-cause sf, ff and Hf share one bound, on \(\log H(t)\), the log of the all-cause cumulative hazard (log(-log sf), the scale of the univariate bounds). As \(H = \sum_k H_k\) and the causes’ parameters are independent, \(\mathrm{Var}\,H = \sum_k \mathrm{Var}\,H_k\), each from its cause’s analytic gradient. A model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows has covariance between causes: there the gradient is over every parameter at once, by central differences as for the cumulative incidence below;

  • the cumulative incidence on the logit scale, \(\log(\mathrm{CIF}/(1 - \mathrm{CIF}))\). It depends on every cause’s parameters through an integral, so its gradient is taken by central differences on covariance_names (each step \(10^{-5}\) of the parameter’s value or standard error, whichever is larger);

  • one cause’s net sf, ff and Hf (event=k) are functions of that cause’s parameters alone: the bound is the cause’s own models[k].cb, which also offers method="lr" and "bootstrap". For a model fitted to left- or interval-censored rows, whose causes were fitted jointly, the Wald bound is the cause’s block of the joint covariance, and the other two are over the joint likelihood: "lr" the extreme of the cause’s cumulative hazard over every cause’s parameters whose deviance from the maximum is within the chi-squared critical value (the univariate fits’ z ** 2; with one cause, the univariate fit’s bound), and "bootstrap" the bias-corrected percentile interval over n_boot case-bootstrap refits: the units resampled with replacement, each with its window, cause and entry, and the causes fitted together again. A resample with no failure of some cause is left out, with a warning counting such resamples. Both need the data, which from_dict does not restore.

The bounds hold at each x separately.

  • bound (('two-sided', 'lower', 'upper'), str, optional) – The two-sided interval (the default), with one row per x and the columns [lower, upper], or one side, which puts all of alpha_ci on that side.

  • method (('wald', 'lr', 'bootstrap'), str, optional) – "wald" (the default). "lr" and "bootstrap" are available for a cause’s net function (event=k, on not "cif"), from its own model or, for a joint fit, from the joint likelihood (above); elsewhere they raise a ValueError saying so.

  • random_state (int or numpy Generator, optional) – The seed of the bootstrap’s simulations (or resamples, for a joint fit, whose refits are kept on the model for each n_boot and integer seed and reused at other times).

cif(x: ArrayLike, event: Any = None) → NDArray | float

Cumulative incidence function. event=k returns \(\int_0^t f_k^{\mathrm{sub}}(u)\,du\), the probability of having failed from cause k by t; event=None gives the all-cause incidence \(1 - S(t) = \sum_k \mathrm{CIF}_k(t)\).

The integral is taken over the cause’s own probability scale,

\[\mathrm{CIF}_k(t) = \int_0^{F_k(t)} \prod_{j \neq k} S_j\left(F_k^{-1}(p)\right) dp,\]

(the substitution \(p = F_k(u)\)), by adaptive quadrature to a relative accuracy of about \(10^{-10}\). The integrand is bounded by 1 and monotone, so the result is accurate for every requested time independently of the others, over any span of times, and also where a cause’s density is infinite (a Weibull shape below 1) or very heavy-tailed. The causes’ CIFs sum to the all-cause ff to that accuracy. Each cause’s model needs a quantile function qf.

  • x (array_like or float) – Times at which to evaluate the incidence; inf gives the eventual probability of the cause (see probability_of_cause()).

  • event (optional) – The cause; None for all causes combined.

Returns:

The cumulative incidence at each time, in the shape of x (a float for a scalar x).

Return type:

numpy array or float

The covariance of every cause’s fitted parameters, in the order of covariance_names: the causes’ own covariance() on the diagonal, and zeros between causes.

The zeros are exact, not an approximation: the competing-risks likelihood is a product of one factor per cause (each cause’s density where it failed, its survival elsewhere), so each cause’s parameters are estimated from their own factor and the information matrix is block diagonal. With left- or interval-censored rows it is not: those rows tie the causes together, the causes are fitted jointly (see fit()), and the covariance is the inverse of the joint Hessian, with the terms between causes; each cause’s covariance() is then its block of it. A cause fitted with lfp=True or zi=True adds its lfp_p or f0 row; an offset gamma has no row (see covariance_names).

Notes

Left truncation keeps the likelihood factorising across causes: a unit entering at \(t_l\) contributes \(\prod_k h_k(x)^{\delta_k} S_k(x) / S_k(t_l)\), so each cause’s distribution is fitted with the same tl.

Left and interval censoring do not factorise. A unit found failed from cause \(k\) in \((x_l, x_r]\) contributes the probability of that, \((\mathrm{CIF}_k(x_r) - \mathrm{CIF}_k(x_l)) / S(t_l)\), an integral of the all-cause survival (a left-censored row is the window from its entry tl, or from the start). So with any such row the causes are fitted together, by maximum likelihood over every cause’s parameters at once (how="MLE" only, continuous distributions); the integral is taken by Gauss-Legendre quadrature on the cause’s probability scale. The covariance is then the inverse of the joint Hessian, with terms between causes (see covariance()), and each cause’s model in models carries the parameters and its block of it, but not the data, so its own neg_ll, aic, likelihood-ratio and bootstrap bounds are not available: use this model’s (cb(x, event=k, on="sf", method="lr") or method="bootstrap", over the joint likelihood). Fitting each cause as a univariate model with the other causes’ events right censored would be an approximation, not this model’s likelihood (it is the starting point of the search). Without such rows the fit is a cause at a time, as before. Right truncation is not taken.

Quantile function. event=None (the default) gives the time by which a fraction p of units has failed from any cause: the inverse of the all-cause ff(), so the B10 life is qf(0.1) and the median qf(0.5). event=k gives cause k’s net quantile (its own model’s qf, the inverse of ff(x, event=k)).

The all-cause failure function has no closed-form inverse, so the quantile is the root of \(H(t) = -\log(1 - p)\), with \(H = \sum_k H_k\) the all-cause cumulative hazard, found by bracketed root finding (Brent’s method) on \(\log t\). The root lies between the causes’ net quantiles: no later than the earliest cause’s quantile at p, and no earlier than the earliest at \(1 - (1 - p)^{1/K}\) for K causes.

class surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.regression.fine_gray.FineGray_

Bases: FitterRepr

The Fine-Gray subdistribution-hazards regression for one cause of a competing-risks problem: the covariates act proportionally on the subdistribution hazard of the cause of interest, so a coefficient describes its effect on that cause’s cumulative incidence directly,

\[F_k(t \mid Z) = 1 - \exp\left(-\Lambda_{k0}(t)\, e^{\beta' Z}\right).\]

Estimated by inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting (IPCW), with one Kaplan-Meier censoring distribution for the whole sample, so censoring is assumed not to depend on the covariates. A subject that failed from a competing cause at \(x_i\) keeps the weight \(\hat G(t-)/\hat G(x_i-)\) at a later event time \(t\): the censoring survival is taken just before each time, so a censoring tied with an event counts after it, as in R’s cmprsk::crr. FineGray (from surpyval.univariate.competing_risks) is an instance of this class; its fit returns a FineGrayModel.

Fit the Fine-Gray model with a ridge, lasso or elastic-net penalty on the coefficients: for many covariates, or correlated ones.

The fit minimises the negative weighted partial log-likelihood of fit() (the subdistribution risk sets with their inverse-probability-of-censoring weights) divided by the number of units N (the sum of n), plus

\[\sum_j \lambda_j \left( \frac{1 - a}{2} b_j^2 + a |b_j| \right),\]

with \(\lambda_j\) the penalizer, \(a\) the l1_ratio and \(b_j\) the coefficient of covariate j divided by its standard deviation (standardise=True): the objective of R’s fastcmprsk::fastCrrp and crrp, and the one every penalised fit in SurPyval minimises (see CoxPH.fit_penalised). A lasso or elastic-net penalty sets some coefficients exactly to 0. With no penalty it is the fit of fit().

With strata each stratum has its own baseline, risk sets and censoring distribution, as for fit(); N counts the units of every stratum, and the covariates are standardised over all the rows.

The model predicts the cumulative incidence as any Fine-Gray model does, but has no covariance, standard errors or p-values (robust or model-based): penalised estimates are biased towards 0 by design, and intervals from them would not have their stated coverage; those calls raise an error that says so (summary() shows them as nan). covariance(robust: bool = True) → NDArray

The coefficients’ covariance (a nan row and column for an aliased coefficient). It was cov before v0.23.

By default it is the robust (sandwich) covariance of Fine and Gray (1999), which R’s cmprsk::crr reports as var: A^-1 M A^-1, A the information of the weighted partial likelihood and M the sum over the units of the squares of their influence on the score, the part through the estimated censoring distribution G included. robust=False gives the model-based covariance A^-1 (crr’s invinf), which takes the censoring weights as known; it was the only covariance before v0.27.

For a fit with sampling weights w the robust covariance is the same sandwich with each unit’s influence (the part through G, the Kaplan-Meier of the censoring weighted by w, included) multiplied by its weight, so w = 1 gives the unweighted covariance; robust=False is refused. A model restored from a dict saved before v0.27 has only the model-based covariance: ask for it with robust=False, or refit.

class surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.regression.competing_risks_proportional_hazard.CompetingRisksProportionalHazards

Bases: PenalisedModelMixin, CompetingRisksPartialEffectsMixin, InformationCriteriaMixin, LinearPredictorMixin, DiagnoseMixin, SerialisableMixin

Competing-risks proportional-hazards regression.

Fits either a cause-specific proportional-hazards model (model="Cox", one Cox model per cause with the other causes treated as censored) or a Fine-Gray subdistribution-hazards model (model="Fine-Gray"). The naming follows the package convention (compare CompetingRisks and ProportionalHazards).

Call the class method CompetingRisksProportionalHazards.fit (or fit_from_df); it returns a fitted instance. Every prediction takes the covariates Z and, for one cause, its label event: an array in the fitted column order or, for a model fitted with fit_from_df, a DataFrame of the raw covariate columns (a formula is applied to it, as for CoxPH). The baselines are step functions, so the interp of sf, ff, Hf, hf and df takes only "step"; another value raises a ValueError. A fitted model can be saved with to_dict/to_json and restored with from_dict/from_json (or surpyval.from_dict).

For model="Cox", log_likelihood is the sum of the causes’ maximised partial log-likelihoods, which is the partial likelihood of the cause-specific hazards model (it factorises by cause), neg_ll() its negative, and aic(), aic_c() and bic() penalise it by the estimated coefficients of every cause, BIC’s sample size being the events of every cause (#604). The Fine-Gray causes’ weighted partial likelihoods are separate estimating functions, not the parts of one likelihood, so for model="Fine-Gray" these raise a ValueError (log_likelihood, a property, an AttributeError, so getattr(model, "log_likelihood", None) works); each cause’s FineGray model has its own.

The coefficients are per cause: betas has one row per cause, in the order of event_idx_map, and params is them flattened, cause by cause, named by parameter_names ("a: grp", the cause and the covariate). covariance() (block-diagonal across the causes, each block that cause’s fit’s), standard_errors(), p_values and summary() describe them, as CoxPH’s do.

class surpyval.univariate.competing_risks.regression.parametric_competing_risks_regression.ParametricCompetingRisksRegression

Bases: ParameterBoundsMixin, DiagnoseMixin, SerialisableMixin

A parametric competing-risks regression: one parametric regression model per cause, each of that cause’s hazard given the covariates, combined into cumulative incidence functions of time and covariates.

Build it from data with fit() or fit_from_df(). The fitted per-cause models are ordinary SurPyval regression models, in models[cause]; causes lists the causes in order. Every function takes the times x and the covariate rows Z as the regressions take them: one row per time, one row used at every time, or one time for every row.

event picks a cause. Without it hf, Hf, sf, ff and df are the all-cause functions (any failure); with it they are the cause’s net ones (its own model, as if the other causes were removed). cif(x, Z, event) is the cumulative incidence: the probability of having failed from that cause by x, with the other causes competing.

diagnose() on the whole model is not built yet and says so: check each cause’s own regression, models[cause].diagnose().

cif(x: ArrayLike, Z: Any, event: Any = None) → NDArray

Cumulative incidence at times x and covariates Z: the probability of having failed from cause event by x, with the other causes competing. event=None gives the all-cause incidence \(1 - S(x \mid Z)\), the sum of the causes’.

It is a smooth function of time defined at every time, past the last observation too; inf gives the eventual probability of the cause (probability_of_cause()). The integral \(\int_0^x h_k S\,du\) is taken over the cause’s own probability scale,

\[\mathrm{CIF}_k(x \mid Z) = \int_0^{F_k(x \mid Z)} \prod_{j \neq k} S_j\left(F_k^{-1}(p \mid Z) \mid Z \right) dp,\]

by adaptive quadrature to a relative accuracy of about \(10^{-10}\), every (time, row) pair at once. The integrand is bounded by 1 and monotone, so the result is as accurate for a cause whose density is infinite at 0 (a Weibull shape below 1) or heavy-tailed as for any other.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like or float) – The times.

  • Z (array_like or DataFrame) – The covariates: one row used at every time, one row per time, or several rows for one time.

  • event (optional) – The cause; None for all causes combined.

Returns:

The cumulative incidence of each (time, row) pair; nan where the time or a covariate is missing.

Return type:

numpy array or scalar

property parameter_bounds: list[tuple[float | None, float | None]]

one (low, high) pair per entry of the vector with_params() takes, in the order of covariance_names (None for an end with no bound).

A parameter set drawn inside these ranges is one with_params does not refuse for being out of range, and one just outside a finite end is refused. Draw a parameter bounded on one side on the log scale of its distance from that end, and one bounded on both on the logit scale between them. Whether an end itself is allowed depends on the parameter (a probability may be 0 where a scale may not), so keep draws strictly inside.

A few models also constrain their parameters jointly, which no per-parameter range can say, and with_params refuses a set that breaks the joint constraint, saying so: a mixture’s weights sum to 1; a Uniform’s a is below its b (as a Beta4’s are); a Royston-Parmar spline does not fall; a Binomial’s n is a whole number. The entry of a parameter with_params ignores (held fixed in the fit, or an accelerated life model’s life parameter) gives the parameter’s range all the same. An accelerated life model’s life-model parameters can have ranges that depend on the stresses fitted to; a model loaded from a dict saved without its data gives them as unbounded.
