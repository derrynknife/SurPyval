Hazard Ratio

Every parametric model takes a hazard ratio hr, a known number above 0, beside its offset gamma, limited-failure proportion lfp_p and zero-inflation fraction f0. A model with hr has hr times the hazard of the same model without it, at every age:

\[H(t) = hr \cdot H_0(t), \qquad R(t) = R_0(t)^{hr},\]

where \(H_0\) and \(R_0\) are the model’s cumulative hazard and survival without hr. Use it for a stress, environment or quality factor that multiplies a known hazard (a part run hotter than the fleet it was fitted on, a handbook’s environment factor), for derating, or to split one life into parts: the models at hr = 1 - b and hr = b together make the model, since \(R^{1-b} R^{b} = R\), which is how a group of units sharing a common cause is built.

>>> import surpyval as surv
>>> base = surv.Weibull.from_params([1000, 2])
>>> hot = surv.Weibull.from_params([1000, 2], hr=1.5)
>>> print(hot)
Parametric SurPyval Model
=========================
Distribution        : Weibull
Fitted by           : given parameters
Hazard ratio (hr)   : 1.5
Parameters          :
     alpha: 1000
      beta: 2
>>> float(hot.sf(500)), float(base.sf(500) ** 1.5)
(0.6872892787909722, 0.6872892787909722)

How it applies

  • Outermost. hr applies over the offset, the limited failure population and the zero inflation: the whole model’s survival is raised to hr. A mass f0 at 0 becomes 1 - (1 - f0) ** hr, which is also df(0) and hf(0); the proportion that never fails becomes (1 - lfp_p) ** hr.

  • Every function. sf, ff, Hf, hf and df follow from the cumulative hazard, so each is precise far into both tails (ff from -expm1(-hr * H0)). A discrete model’s hazard is 1 - (1 - h0) ** hr. qf(p) and isf(s) invert through the model’s isf at \(R_0 = R^{1/hr}\), computed in log space, and random draws qf of its uniforms, so isf(sf(t)) gives back t at a survival of 1e-300.

  • Closed forms stay exact. A Weibull with hr is the Weibull with scale \(\alpha\, hr^{-1/\beta}\), and an exponential’s rate is \(hr \cdot \lambda\) (an offset and zero inflation carry across): their mean, moment, var, entropy, mrl and restricted mean are those families’ closed forms. Other distributions integrate the survival function numerically for the mean, moments, variance, mean residual life and restricted mean, and refuse the entropy (integrate -df * log(df) instead). A discrete model’s moments, and the defective moments of a limited failure population, are refused with what to use.

  • ``hr = 1`` changes nothing. Every function and every seeded draw is the model’s without hr, to the last bit.

hr is not a parameter: it is not in params, covariance_names or k. It is in extras (so dist.from_params(params, **model.extras) keeps it), with_params(..., hr=) sets it on any model, and rescale_time keeps it (a hazard ratio has no unit).

Distributions that are probabilities of an event (Bernoulli, Binomial, FixedEventProbability, ExactEventTime) refuse it: scale the probability itself, q becoming 1 - (1 - q) ** hr.

Fitting with a known hazard ratio

fit(..., hr=) fits data that come from an environment with a known hazard ratio: the likelihood is the hazard-scaled model’s, and the fitted model carries hr. It is never estimated, since it is confounded with the scale (a Weibull fitted with hr = 2 is the plain fit with its scale multiplied by \(2^{1/\beta}\)). Maximum likelihood only.

>>> x = [10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 25, 31]
>>> plain = surv.Weibull.fit(x)
>>> hot = surv.Weibull.fit(x, hr=2.0)
>>> hot.hr, hot.params.round(3)
(2.0, array([26.451,  2.931]))
>>> round(float(plain.params[0] * 2 ** (1 / plain.params[1])), 3)
26.451

The bounds hold hr fixed, as a known constant:

  • cb and quantile_cb with method="wald" (every on= target) and method="bootstrap" (each refit has the same hr);

  • param_cb with every method, the likelihood-ratio bounds included (the profile likelihood is the hazard-scaled model’s);

  • refused, naming what to use instead: the likelihood-ratio bounds on a function (cb/quantile_cb with method="lr"), and mean_cb, mrl_cb, rmst_cb and rmst (bound them from the bootstrap refits, model.bootstrap(...).models, which carry hr).

Saving

to_dict writes "hr" only where it is not 1, and such a dict is stamped schema 5, which SurPyval 0.28 and older refuse with a request to upgrade rather than load the model without its hazard ratio. A model with hr = 1 saves as before.
