Shared Frailty Models

A frailty model adds an unobserved multiplicative random effect shared by every observation in a group, so that units from the same group are correlated:

\[h(x \mid Z, w) = w \, h_0(x) \, e^{\beta' Z}\]

with the frailty \(w\) drawn once per group from a distribution with mean one and variance \(\theta\). Setting \(\theta = 0\) recovers the ordinary proportional-hazards model, so the fitted \(\theta\) measures how much of the variation is between groups rather than within them.

Use this when observations arrive in clusters that share something you have not measured – repairs on the same machine, patients at the same hospital, components from the same batch. Treating them as independent understates the uncertainty.

Factory:

from surpyval import Frailty, Weibull
model = Frailty(Weibull).fit(x, Z=Z, c=c, groups=unit_id)

Pre-built instances: ExponentialFrailty, WeibullFrailty, LogNormalFrailty, GammaFrailty.

The frailty is Gamma-distributed by default, so it integrates out of each group’s likelihood in closed form. Frailty(dist, family="lognormal") takes a log-normal frailty instead, \(w = e^{v}\) with \(v \sim N(0, \theta)\) (the parameterisation of R’s frailtypack and coxme: theta is then the variance of the log frailty, and the median frailty is 1), whose group integrals are computed by adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature with 30 nodes (accurate to about \(10^{-10}\) in each group’s log-likelihood for \(\theta \le 1\), \(10^{-7}\) at 2 and \(10^{-5}\) at 5). frailty_variance (the variance of the frailty scaled to mean 1) and kendall_tau put the two families on one scale. Only observed and right-censored data are supported, and at least two groups are needed; a row with a missing group label (None, NaN) is dropped, with a warning, and a missing group= at prediction gives nan. The fitted model predicts the marginal (population) curve by default, or the curve conditional on an observed group’s posterior frailty (group=) or on a given frailty (frailty=). Like the parametric regression models it reports neg_ll(), aic(), bic() and aic_c() (theta counted as a parameter), so a frailty fit can be compared directly with the proportional-hazards fit it reduces to at theta = 0. fit_penalised adds a ridge, lasso or elastic-net penalty on the coefficients (the baseline and theta are not penalised); where its objective is smallest at theta = 0 it is the penalised proportional-hazards fit (WeibullPH.fit_penalised for WeibullFrailty). See the penalised regression section of Regression Modelling with SurPyval.

CoxFrailty fits the gamma frailty with the baseline left unspecified (a Cox baseline), by EM over the frailties with theta maximising the profile likelihood – the fit of R’s coxph(... + frailty(id, dist = "gamma")):

from surpyval import CoxFrailty
model = CoxFrailty.fit(x, Z=Z, c=c, groups=unit_id)

It predicts like the parametric models, from a step baseline as CoxPH does, and reports R’s integrated likelihood (its loglik) as log_likelihood, which aic() and bic() penalise.

CoxFrailty.fit(..., family="lognormal") fits the log-normal frailty with a Cox baseline instead: each group’s frailty is \(e^{v}\) with \(v \sim N(0, \theta)\), a random intercept on the log hazard. It is fitted by maximum likelihood as R’s coxme(... + (1 | id)): for a given \(\theta\) the coefficients and each group’s \(v\) maximise the penalised partial likelihood, and \(\theta\) maximises its Laplace approximation to the integrated partial likelihood, reported as log_likelihood. frailties are \(e^{\hat v}\), each group’s predicted (posterior-mode) log-frailty exponentiated; theta’s standard error is from the curvature of its profile likelihood. R’s coxph(... + frailty(id, dist = "gaussian")) fits the same penalised partial likelihood but chooses \(\theta\) by a different rule (an approximate REML rather than maximum likelihood); at the same \(\theta\) (theta=) the two fits agree.

Mixed-effects Cox models

When the groups come in more than one layer – wards within hospitals, or batches crossed with machines – or when the effect of a covariate itself differs from group to group, use CoxMixedEffects, the counterpart of R’s coxme. In plain words: every grouping factor gives each of its groups a random multiplier on the hazard (and, if you ask, a random change to a covariate’s effect), drawn from a normal distribution on the log scale whose variance the fit estimates. A group with few events is pulled towards the average (shrinkage). The fixed coefficients compare two units of the same groups.

The model is

\[h_i(t) = h_0(t) \exp\Big(\beta' Z_i + \sum_{\text{terms } t} v_{ti}' b_{t, g_t(i)}\Big), \qquad b_{t, g} \sim N(0, \Sigma_t),\]

where v holds a 1 (a random intercept) and any random-slope covariates, and \(b_{t,g}\) are group g’s random effects for term t, independent between groups and between terms. The fit is coxme’s (Ripatti and Palmgren 2000; Therneau, Grambsch and Pankratz 2003): for given variances, \(\beta\) and the \(b\) maximise the penalised partial likelihood \(PL(\beta, b) - \tfrac12 b' \Sigma^{-1} b\); the variances maximise the Laplace approximation to the integrated partial likelihood. Ties are Efron’s or Breslow’s, and counts, late entry (tl) and strata are taken as CoxPH takes them.

The random effects are given with groups= (a random intercept), with RandomEffect terms, or in fit_from_df with the bar notation of coxme and lme4:

from surpyval import CoxMixedEffects, RandomEffect

# a random intercept per centre
model = CoxMixedEffects.fit(x, Z=Z, c=c, groups=centre)
# a correlated random intercept and slope of column 0 per centre
model = CoxMixedEffects.fit(
    x, Z=Z, c=c, random_effects=RandomEffect(centre, slopes=[0])
)
# wards nested in hospitals, crossed with batches
model = CoxMixedEffects.fit(
    x, Z=Z, c=c,
    random_effects=[
        RandomEffect(hospital),
        RandomEffect((hospital, ward)),
        RandomEffect(batch),
    ],
)
# the same in a DataFrame
model = CoxMixedEffects.fit_from_df(
    df, x_col="time", c_col="censored", Z_cols=["trt", "age"],
    random_effects="(1 | hospital/ward) + (1 | batch)",
)

In the bar notation (1 | g) is a random intercept, (1 + trt | g) a correlated intercept and slope, (0 + trt | g) a slope alone, (1 + trt || g) an independent intercept and slope, and (1 | a/b) b nested in a. A slope must say whether the term has an intercept: (trt | g) is refused, because lme4 and coxme read it differently. A random slope is a covariate of the fixed part too.

The model reports what coxme reports. beta, summary() and param_cb give the fixed coefficients with Wald intervals, from the inverse of the penalised information. variance_components() gives each term’s variances, standard deviations and correlations; variance_cb() their profile-likelihood intervals (a lower bound of 0 where the data cannot rule a variance of 0 out); random_effects each group’s predicted random effects (coxme’s ranef). log_likelihood is the integrated log-likelihood (the one aic() and bic() penalise, by the fixed coefficients and the variance parameters), log_likelihood_penalised and log_likelihood_fitted the penalised partial likelihood at the fit with and without its penalty, log_likelihood_null its value at \(\beta = 0, b = 0\) and df_penalised the penalised fit’s effective degrees of freedom. lr_test() tests all the random effects against the Cox model: a variance of 0 is on the edge of its range, so with one variance the p-value is half the chi-square’s (the 50:50 mixture), with several independent variances the binomial mixture, and with correlations the plain chi-square, which is conservative.

Predictions (sf, Hf, ff, hf, df, qf) are marginal by default, as CoxFrailty’s: the random effects of every term not named in group= are averaged over, the curve of a unit from a group not yet seen. group=label (group={"hospital": 3, "batch": 2} with several terms) conditions on an observed group’s predicted random effects, and marginal=False sets the remaining random effects to 0 (a typical group, the curve coxme predicts). stratum= picks a stratum’s baseline. The baseline is a step function, as CoxPH’s.

A worked example, the lung cancer data with a random intercept per institution:

>>> from surpyval import CoxMixedEffects
>>> from surpyval.datasets import load_lung
>>> df = load_lung()
>>> df["censored"] = 1 - df["status"]
>>> model = CoxMixedEffects.fit_from_df(
...     df, x_col="time", c_col="censored", Z_cols=["age", "sex"],
...     random_effects="(1 | inst)",
... )
>>> model.lr_test()["p_value"]
1.0
>>> model.variance_cb().round(3)
                       estimate  lower  upper
term parameter
inst var((Intercept))       0.0    0.0  0.104

The institutions do not differ detectably: the variance is 0 (the fit is CoxPH’s, on the 227 rows with an institution), and the data rule out a variance above about 0.10, a standard deviation of 0.32 in the log hazard. (One row has no institution and is dropped, with a warning.)

Not available: random() (the baseline is a step function: use a parametric frailty model), diagnose() (test the random effects with lr_test(), and check the fixed part with CoxPH.fit(...).diagnose()), weights, and interval- or left-censored data (use Frailty(Weibull, family="lognormal")). The random-effects design is held as a dense matrix, so a model with many thousands of groups is slow.

surpyval.univariate.regression.frailty.Frailty(distribution: Any, family: str = 'gamma') → FrailtyFitter

Create a shared-frailty proportional-hazards fitter for a distribution.

A shared-frailty model adds a random hazard multiplier shared within a group (a lot, site, or repairable unit) on top of a proportional-hazards baseline, capturing unobserved between-group heterogeneity and the within-group correlation it induces. The frailty is Gamma by default, whose closed-form marginal likelihood keeps the fit fast, or log-normal (family="lognormal"), integrated out by adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature.

Parameters:

  • distribution (ParametricFitter) – A surpyval parametric distribution (e.g. Weibull, Exponential).

  • family (str, optional) – The frailty distribution: "gamma" (the default), mean 1 and variance theta; or "lognormal", u = exp(w) with w normal of mean 0 and variance theta, the parameterisation of R’s frailtypack (RandDist = "LogN") and coxme. frailty_variance (Var(u) / E(u)^2) and kendall_tau compare the two; so does aic().

Returns:

A fitter with .fit(x, Z, c, groups=...) and .fit_from_df.

Return type:

FrailtyFitter

Examples

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from surpyval import Frailty, Weibull
>>> np.random.seed(1)
>>> unit_id = np.repeat(np.arange(20), 5)
>>> Z = np.random.binomial(1, 0.5, 100).reshape(-1, 1)
>>> x = Weibull.random(100, 10, 2) * np.exp(-0.5 * Z[:, 0])
>>> model = Frailty(Weibull).fit(x, Z=Z, groups=unit_id)
>>> model.beta.round(3)
array([0.829])
>>> model.n_groups
20

fit_penalised(x: Any, Z: Any, c: Any = None, n: Any = None, groups: Any = None, penalizer: Any = 0.1, l1_ratio: float = 0.0, standardise: bool = True, tol: float = 1e-10, w: Any = None, *, unit=None) → FrailtyModel

Fit the shared-frailty model with a ridge, lasso or elastic-net penalty on the coefficients (the baseline’s parameters and the frailty variance theta are not penalised): for many covariates, or correlated ones.

The fit minimises the negative marginal log-likelihood of fit() (the frailty integrated out) divided by the number of units N (the sum of n), plus

\[\sum_j \lambda_j \left( \frac{1 - a}{2} b_j^2 + a |b_j| \right),\]

with \(\lambda_j\) the penalizer, \(a\) the l1_ratio and \(b_j\) the coefficient of covariate j divided by its standard deviation (standardise=True), as every penalised fit in SurPyval (see WeibullPH.fit_penalised).

theta = 0, no shared frailty, is the edge of its space, where the model is the proportional hazards model of the same baseline. Where the objective is smallest there, theta is 0 and the fit is the penalised proportional hazards fit (WeibullPH.fit_penalised for WeibullFrailty), with the objective checked not to fall as theta leaves 0. With no penalty it is the fit of fit().

The model predicts as the fit’s does (the marginal curve by default, a group’s with group=), but has no covariance, standard errors, bounds or p-values: penalised estimates are biased towards 0 by design, and intervals from them would not have their stated coverage; those calls raise an error that says so (summary() shows them as nan). Its aic and bic count the effective degrees of freedom (the baseline’s parameters and theta one each).

Parameters:

  • x – As for fit(); Z is required (with no covariates there is nothing to penalise: use fit()).

  • Z – As for fit(); Z is required (with no covariates there is nothing to penalise: use fit()).

  • c – As for fit(); Z is required (with no covariates there is nothing to penalise: use fit()).

  • n – As for fit(); Z is required (with no covariates there is nothing to penalise: use fit()).

  • groups – As for fit(); Z is required (with no covariates there is nothing to penalise: use fit()).

  • penalizer (float or array_like, optional) – \(\lambda\): one value, or one per column of Z (0 leaves a coefficient unpenalised). Default 0.1.

  • l1_ratio (float, optional) – \(a\), from 0 (ridge, the default) to 1 (lasso).

  • standardise (bool, optional) – True (the default) penalises the coefficients of the covariates divided by their standard deviations (count- weighted, divisor N, as glmnet), so the penalty does not depend on their units; the coefficients are reported on the covariates’ own scale either way.

  • tol (float, optional) – The optimality tolerance on the gradient of the objective.

  • w (None) – Weights are refused: fit() takes none either (give repeated rows as counts n).

Returns:

The fitted model, with penalty (the penalizer per column, l1_ratio, standardise, the columns’ scale and the effective degrees of freedom).

Return type:

FrailtyModel

class surpyval.univariate.regression.frailty.frailty_model.FrailtyModel

Bases: _SharedFrailty

A fitted shared-frailty proportional-hazards model.

See FrailtyFitter for how one is produced. Prediction methods (sf(), ff(), hf(), Hf(), df()) return the marginal (population) curve by default; pass group= to condition on an observed group’s posterior frailty, or frailty= to condition on a supplied frailty value.

neg_ll(), aic(), bic() and aic_c() use the marginal likelihood and count every estimated parameter (baseline, coefficients and theta), on the same data conventions as the parametric regression models, so a frailty fit can be compared directly with the proportional-hazards fit (WeibullPH for WeibullFrailty) of the same data – the model it reduces to at theta = 0.

params is every estimated parameter in one vector, in the order of parameter_names: the baseline distribution’s parameters, then the covariate coefficients (each named by its covariate’s column, else coef_0, coef_1, …; #614), then the frailty variance theta – the order of standard_errors() and of the stored covariance. dist_params, beta and theta hold the same values by part.

Notes

The correction \((2k^2 + 2k)/(N - k - 1)\) uses the same sample size \(N\) as bic(): the number of observed failures (exact, left- or interval-censored, weighted by their counts), or the number of units when there is none. concordance(x: ArrayLike | None = None, c: ArrayLike | None = None, Z: Any = None, tie_tol: float = 1e-08, ties: str = 'therneau', *, weighting: str = 'harrell', tau: float | None = None, alpha_ci: float = 0.05, ci_method: str = 'normal') → ConcordanceResult | float

Harrell’s (or Uno’s) concordance index (C) of the model’s risk scores, with its standard error and confidence interval.

The proportion of usable pairs of subjects – the one with the earlier time had the event – in which the model gives that subject the higher risk: 1 ranks every pair correctly, 0.5 is chance. With no arguments it scores the data the model was fitted to (each row counted n times); pass x, c and Z together to score other data, such as a test set. Two events at the same time are not a pair (ties="therneau", as R’s concordance and lifelines) unless ties="harrell"; see surpyval.metrics.concordance_index() for the treatment of tied times and scores.

The risk score, higher meaning an earlier event, is for each family:

  • CoxPH, the frailty models and AdditiveHazards (Lin-Ying): the linear predictor \(\beta'Z\) (the log hazard ratio, or the covariates’ additive hazard), as R’s concordance and lifelines use;

  • the parametric families (PH, AFT, PO, AH and the accelerated life models): the cumulative hazard \(H(t^* \mid Z)\) at \(t^*\), the median of the times scored. For every family whose covariates act through a linear predictor this ranks the rows exactly as the linear predictor does, with the sign that means a higher risk (\(\beta'Z\) for PH, AFT and AH, \(-\beta'Z\) for PO), and \(t^*\) does not matter; it can only matter for an accelerated life model whose life model sets a shape parameter;

  • BuckleyJames: \(-\beta'Z\), since the model is linear in the log time;

  • ProportionalOdds: \(-\beta'Z\), the log of the odds of failure relative to the baseline’s.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like, optional) – Observed times.

  • c (array_like, optional) – Censoring flags, 0 an event and 1 right censored.

  • Z (array_like or DataFrame, optional) – Covariates, one row per time (a DataFrame for a model fitted with fit_from_df).

  • tie_tol (float, optional) – Scores within this of each other are tied. Default 1e-8.

  • ties ({"therneau", "harrell"}, optional) – "therneau" (the default, as R’s survival::concordance and lifelines): two events at the same time are not a usable pair. "harrell" (Harrell’s original definition): they are, counting 1 if their scores are tied, else 0.5.

  • weighting ({"harrell", "uno"}, optional) – "harrell" (default): every usable pair weighs 1. "uno": Uno’s inverse-probability-of-censoring weighted C, each pair weighted by 1 / G(t-)^2 (see surpyval.metrics.concordance_index()).

  • tau (float, optional) – Count only the pairs whose earlier time is at most tau. Default None, every pair.

  • alpha_ci (float, optional) – Keyword only: the significance level of the interval. Default 0.05.

  • ci_method ({"normal", "logit"}, optional) – Keyword only: the scale of the interval. Default "normal".

Returns:

The concordance index, a float that carries its infinitesimal-jackknife standard error se and interval ci (surpyval.metrics.concordance_index()). With no data it scores the data the model was fitted to, each row repeated n times as its own subject (as R does with the rows repeated); the standard error treats the scores as fixed, as R’s does. A plain nan where a covariate is missing.

Return type:

ConcordanceResult

Raises:

ValueError – If only some of x, c and Z are given, or none are and the model does not keep its training data (a model restored with from_dict, or one fitted with time-varying covariates, whose rows are intervals of a subject’s history).

param_cb(name: str, alpha_ci: float = 0.05, bound: str = 'two-sided', method: str | None = 'wald') → ndarray

Confidence bound(s) on a named parameter.

Two methods, as for the parametric regression models; "wald" is the default:

  • "wald" – from the stored covariance, on a scale chosen from the parameter’s support (log for the positive baseline parameters and theta, natural for the unbounded coefficients) so the interval stays valid.

  • "lr" – the profile-likelihood (likelihood-ratio) interval (#617): the values whose profile deviance of the marginal likelihood, every other parameter re-fitted, stays below the \(\chi^2_1\) critical value (aliases "likelihood", "likelihood-ratio", "profile"). It need not be symmetric about the estimate; where the deviance stays below the critical value to the edge of the space (theta down to 0, no detectable frailty), the bound is that edge. A side that cannot be found is nan, with a warning. It needs the data the model was fitted to, which a model restored from a dict does not keep.

theta = 0 is the edge of its space: where the true theta is 0 the deviance of theta is half a point mass at 0 and half a \(\chi^2_1\) (Self and Liang 1987), so the likelihood-ratio interval, which takes the \(\chi^2_1\) critical value, is conservative there.

Parameters:

  • name (str) – One of parameter_names.

  • alpha_ci (float, optional) – Total tail probability of the bound(s). Default 0.05.

  • bound ({'two-sided', 'lower', 'upper'}, optional) – Two-sided bounds are returned as [lower, upper].

  • method ({'wald', 'lr'} or None, optional) – As above. Default 'wald', which None also means.

class surpyval.univariate.regression.frailty.cox_frailty.CoxFrailtyFitter

Bases: FitterRepr

The shared gamma frailty model with a Cox (unspecified) baseline: the semi-parametric counterpart of Frailty(), as CoxPH is of WeibullPH.

\[h(t \mid Z, u) = u \, h_0(t) \, e^{\beta' Z},\]

with the frailty u shared by every observation of a group, gamma of mean 1 and variance theta, and h_0 left to the data. Fitted by EM over the frailties with theta maximising the profile likelihood, which gives R’s coxph(Surv(time, status) ~ ... + frailty(id, dist = "gamma")) (see fit()). CoxFrailty is an instance.

fit(x: Any, Z: Any = None, c: Any = None, n: Any = None, groups: Any = None, tie_method: str = 'efron', theta: float | None = None, center: bool = False, family: str = 'gamma', *, unit=None) → CoxFrailtyModel

Fit the shared frailty Cox model (gamma, or log-normal with family="lognormal").

For a given frailty variance theta the coefficients, the baseline and each group’s frailty come from EM: the E-step is each group’s posterior mean frailty (closed form for the gamma), the M-step a CoxPH fit with the log-frailties as offsets and the frailty-weighted Breslow baseline (Klein 1992; Nielsen et al. 1992). theta maximises the profile of the integrated likelihood (R’s “I-likelihood”). The fit is that of R’s coxph(Surv(time, status) ~ Z + frailty(groups, dist = "gamma"), ties = tie_method), whose penalised partial likelihood at a given theta has the EM fit as its maximum (Therneau, Grambsch and Pankratz 2003).

Parameters:

  • x (array_like) – The observed times.

  • Z (array_like, optional) – Covariates, one row per observation. Omit for frailty alone. Rows with a missing or infinite covariate are dropped, with a warning; a column the partial likelihood cannot determine (a constant, or a combination of the others) is aliased as in CoxPH: its coefficient is nan, with a warning.

  • c (array_like, optional) – Censoring flags: 0 event, 1 right-censored (the only two supported). Defaults to all events.

  • n (array_like, optional) – Counts of each row. Defaults to 1.

  • groups (array_like) – The group (cluster) of each row; at least two. Rows with a missing label are dropped, with a warning.

  • tie_method (str, optional) – "efron" (the default, as for every Cox fit) or "breslow": the partial likelihood of the M-step and the baseline’s increments, as in CoxPH.

  • theta (float, optional) – A fixed frailty variance (R’s frailty(..., theta = )), in place of its maximum likelihood estimate; it then has no standard error. 0 is the Cox model.

  • center (bool, optional) – False (the default) reports the baseline at Z = 0, as CoxPH does. True keeps it at the (count-weighted) covariate means, stored as model.center, and predicts with exp(beta'(Z - center)) (as R’s coxph): the same model, and the way to fit one whose beta'Z at Z = 0 is beyond what exp can represent (a coefficient running off on covariates far from 0), which is refused at Z = 0 (#794). The coefficients, theta and the frailties are the same either way.

  • family (str, optional) – "gamma" (the default; the fit described above) or "lognormal": u = exp(w) with w ~ N(0, theta), a random intercept on the log-hazard, fitted as R’s coxme(... + (1 | groups)) (maximum likelihood: the coefficients and each group’s w maximise the penalised partial likelihood, and theta its Laplace approximation to the integrated partial likelihood; see CoxMixedEffectsFitter, which fits several terms and random slopes). frailties are then exp of each group’s predicted w (its posterior mode), and log_likelihood the Laplace-approximated integrated partial likelihood.

Returns:

The coefficients beta, the frailty variance theta, each group’s posterior frailty and the baseline at Z = 0 (or at center).

Return type:

CoxFrailtyModel

Bases: _SharedFrailty

A fitted shared gamma frailty model with a Cox baseline, returned by CoxFrailtyFitter.fit().

beta are the coefficients (hazard ratios exp(beta) within a group, given the frailty), theta the frailty variance, and frailties each group’s posterior mean frailty, keyed by its label as a string. x, h0 and H0 are the baseline’s times, its increments and its cumulative hazard, of a unit at Z = 0 with frailty 1 (Breslow’s estimator with the frailties as weights, with Efron’s increments after an Efron fit), as CoxPH reports them.

The prediction methods give the marginal (population) curve by default, S(t | Z) = (1 + theta e^{beta'Z} H_0(t))^{-1/theta}; group= conditions on an observed group’s posterior frailty, and frailty= on a given one, S = exp(-u e^{beta'Z} H_0(t)). As for CoxPH the baseline is a step function: hf and df are the jumps at the latest baseline time at or before x, the survival is 1 before the first time and holds its last value after the last.

params is beta then theta, in the order of parameter_names and of covariance(). The coefficients’ standard errors are R’s (the inverse of the penalised partial likelihood’s information at the estimated theta, with every frailty in it – coxph’s sparse = FALSE); theta’s is from the curvature of its profile likelihood, and its interval (param_cb()) is formed on the log scale. log_likelihood is the integrated log-likelihood (R’s “I-likelihood”) and log_likelihood_no_frailty the Cox partial likelihood, its value at theta = 0: twice their difference is the likelihood-ratio statistic for a frailty, whose null distribution is the 50:50 mixture of 0 and a chi-square on one degree of freedom (theta is on its boundary under the null). (Before v0.23 they were loglik and loglik_no_frailty.)

neg_ll() is the negative integrated log-likelihood, and aic(), aic_c() and bic() penalise it by the estimated parameters k: the coefficients and theta (unless it was fixed), BIC’s sample size being the events (#604). R’s AIC(coxph(... + frailty(id))) differs: it penalises the partial likelihood at the penalised fit by the frailty term’s effective degrees of freedom. These compare Cox frailty models (with each other, and with CoxPH’s partial likelihood, the value at theta = 0), not with a parametric model.

concordance(x: ArrayLike | None = None, c: ArrayLike | None = None, Z: Any = None, tie_tol: float = 1e-08, ties: str = 'therneau', *, weighting: str = 'harrell', tau: float | None = None, alpha_ci: float = 0.05, ci_method: str = 'normal') → ConcordanceResult | float

Harrell’s (or Uno’s) concordance index (C) of the model’s risk scores, with its standard error and confidence interval.

The proportion of usable pairs of subjects – the one with the earlier time had the event – in which the model gives that subject the higher risk: 1 ranks every pair correctly, 0.5 is chance. With no arguments it scores the data the model was fitted to (each row counted n times); pass x, c and Z together to score other data, such as a test set. Two events at the same time are not a pair (ties="therneau", as R’s concordance and lifelines) unless ties="harrell"; see surpyval.metrics.concordance_index() for the treatment of tied times and scores.

The risk score, higher meaning an earlier event, is for each family:

  • CoxPH, the frailty models and AdditiveHazards (Lin-Ying): the linear predictor \(\beta'Z\) (the log hazard ratio, or the covariates’ additive hazard), as R’s concordance and lifelines use;

  • the parametric families (PH, AFT, PO, AH and the accelerated life models): the cumulative hazard \(H(t^* \mid Z)\) at \(t^*\), the median of the times scored. For every family whose covariates act through a linear predictor this ranks the rows exactly as the linear predictor does, with the sign that means a higher risk (\(\beta'Z\) for PH, AFT and AH, \(-\beta'Z\) for PO), and \(t^*\) does not matter; it can only matter for an accelerated life model whose life model sets a shape parameter;

  • BuckleyJames: \(-\beta'Z\), since the model is linear in the log time;

  • ProportionalOdds: \(-\beta'Z\), the log of the odds of failure relative to the baseline’s.

  • ci_method ({"normal", "logit"}, optional) – Keyword only: the scale of the interval. Default "normal".

The mixed-effects Cox model (R’s coxme): a Cox model whose log-hazard has Gaussian random effects, a random intercept per group of one or more grouping factors (nested or crossed) and random slopes, correlated within a group. CoxMixedEffects is an instance; see fit().

Fit the mixed-effects Cox model by maximum likelihood, as R’s coxme.

In words: each group (a hospital, a batch, a machine) gets its own multiplier on the hazard, and possibly its own effect of a covariate, drawn from a normal distribution on the log scale whose variance is estimated. Groups with few events are pulled towards the average (shrinkage), and the fixed coefficients are the effects within a group.

For given variances the coefficients beta and the random effects maximise the penalised partial likelihood PL(beta, b) - b' Sigma^{-1} b / 2 (Newton’s method); the variances maximise the integrated partial likelihood, the random effects integrated out by the Laplace approximation (Ripatti and Palmgren 2000), as coxme does.

Parameters:

  • x (array_like) – The observed times (exits, with tl).

  • Z (array_like, optional) – Fixed covariates, one row per observation. Rows with a missing or infinite covariate are dropped, with a warning; a column the partial likelihood cannot determine is aliased as in CoxPH.

  • c (array_like, optional) – 0 event, 1 right-censored (the only two supported).

  • n (array_like, optional) – Counts of each row.

  • tl (array_like, optional) – Entry (left-truncation) times, as in CoxPH: start-stop rows of a unit keep their group.

  • groups (array_like, optional) – The group of each row: a random intercept per group (R’s (1 | groups)). The shorthand for random_effects=RandomEffect(groups).

  • random_effects (RandomEffect or list of RandomEffect, optional) – The random-effects terms: several grouping factors (nested or crossed), and random slopes. See RandomEffect.

  • strata (array_like, optional) – Stratum labels: a baseline per stratum, as in CoxPH. Predictions then take stratum=.

  • variance (list, optional) – Fixed variances in place of their estimates (R’s vfixed): one entry per term, a variance for a one-effect term or the effects’ covariance matrix. The model then has no variance bounds, and lr_test compares that fixed model.

  • center (bool, optional) – As for CoxPH: True keeps the baseline at the covariate means.

class surpyval.univariate.regression.frailty.cox_mixed_effects.CoxMixedEffectsModel

Bases: ConditionalSurvivalMixin, InformationCriteriaMixin, DiagnoseMixin, SerialisableMixin

A fitted mixed-effects Cox model, returned by CoxMixedEffectsFitter.fit().

beta are the fixed coefficients: exp(beta) is the hazard ratio of two units of the same group (with the same random effects). Their covariance is the inverse of the penalised partial likelihood’s information, its fixed block (coxme’s vcov), with Wald intervals (param_cb(), summary()).

variance_components() gives each term’s standard deviations, variances and correlations, and variance_cb() their profile-likelihood intervals; lr_test() tests the random effects together against the Cox model without them. random_effects are the predicted random effects of each group (the posterior modes, coxme’s ranef: best linear unbiased predictions, shrunk towards 0).

The log-likelihoods are coxme’s: log_likelihood the integrated (Laplace) partial likelihood, the one maximised and the one aic() and bic() penalise (by k, the fixed coefficients and the variance parameters, as coxme’s integrated AIC); log_likelihood_penalised the penalised partial likelihood at the fit, log_likelihood_fitted the partial likelihood there without the penalty (coxme prints it as “Fitted”), log_likelihood_null at beta = 0 and b = 0, and log_likelihood_no_random the Cox model’s (CoxPH) on the same rows; df_penalised is the penalised fit’s effective degrees of freedom.

Predictions are marginal by default, as CoxFrailty’s: the random effects of every term not named in group= are integrated out (the curve of a unit from a group not yet seen). group= (a label, or a {term: label} dict with several terms) conditions on an observed group’s predicted random effects; marginal=False sets the remaining random effects to 0 instead (a typical, median group), the curve coxme predicts. The baseline is a step function, as CoxPH’s: hf and df are its jumps.

lr_test() → dict

The likelihood-ratio test of the random effects together: twice the integrated log-likelihood less the Cox model’s partial log-likelihood (CoxPH on the same rows), and its p-value.

Every variance is 0 under the null, on the edge of its range, so the statistic is not a plain chi-square. With one variance (a random intercept) it is the 50:50 mixture of 0 and a chi-square on 1 degree of freedom (Self and Liang 1987); with m independent variances (no correlations) the binomial mixture of chi-squares on 0 to m degrees of freedom. With correlations the exact mixture depends on the information, and the plain chi-square on the number of variance parameters is used: its p-value is larger than the true one (conservative), which reference says. A model fitted with variance= estimated no variance: its statistic is reported with a nan p-value.
