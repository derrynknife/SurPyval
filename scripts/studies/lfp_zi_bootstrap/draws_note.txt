Bootstrap coverage of limited-failure (lfp) and zero-inflated (zi) fits
with the current way of reading bounds from the refits, and at 200 against
500 refits.

Why. The calibration figures recorded for these bounds (90% intervals
0.835-0.925, 95% 0.90-0.965) and the 400-replicate comparison with a case
bootstrap (results_note.txt) were measured before bootstrap bounds were
read at the (n + 1) p-th refit instead of the (n - 1) p-th, a change that
widened every bootstrap interval a little and added about a point of
coverage to the plain models'. They had not been measured since. And the
question left open there: do more refits (n_boot above 200) close the
remaining shortfall?

Run. draws.py 200 4, then draws_summary.py (4 cores, about 2.5 hours;
35,000 core-seconds: lfp_edge 14,954, lognormal_lfp 8,204, lfp 7,314,
zi 4,528). Replicate r of each design is the r-th data set drawn from the
calibration study's seed (311-314), so the 200 replicates are its data
exactly, and the bounds are the public param_cb, cb and quantile_cb with
the calibration's random_state = 1000 * seed + r. The 200-refit bounds
are therefore what test_bootstrap_coverage computes on this code. The 500
refits extend the 200 (one stream of resamples: the first 200 of the 500
are the 200, checked on two replicates per design and by
test_bootstrap_bounds.py::test_286_more_refits_for_a_seed_extend_the_fewer),
so the two counts are paired. No refit failed. Per-cell figures, with the
paired counts and median widths, are in draws_summary.json.

Monte Carlo error of one cell at 200 replicates: about 0.021 at 0.90 and
0.015 at 0.95. The calibration rule's tolerance is 0.074 and 0.056.

1. Every cell passes the calibration rule at both counts. Mean over the
   35 cells whose target is the nominal level (the edge design's lfp_p
   left out, as the calibration's pooled report leaves it out):

                     90% interval            95% interval
   design         n_boot 200  n_boot 500  n_boot 200  n_boot 500
   lfp               0.904       0.913       0.951       0.961
   lfp_edge          0.877       0.867       0.935       0.937
   zi                0.871       0.871       0.930       0.932
   lognormal_lfp     0.898       0.906       0.944       0.951
   all               0.888       0.890       0.940       0.945

   Cell ranges at n_boot 200: 90% lfp 0.870-0.935, lfp_edge 0.855-0.890,
   zi 0.845-0.910, lognormal_lfp 0.845-0.920; 95% lfp 0.940-0.965,
   lfp_edge 0.905-0.970, zi 0.915-0.950, lognormal_lfp 0.915-0.965. The
   edge design's lfp_p covered 0.995 (target 0.95) and 0.995 (0.975).

2. The (n + 1) p rule moved these designs as it moved the plain ones: the
   lfp and LogNormal lfp designs are now at their level. The edge and zi
   designs are still about 2.5-3 points short at 90% and 1.5-2 at 95%,
   in every kind of bound (parameters, sf and quantiles); the lowest cells
   are the Weibull scale alpha (0.855 and 0.86 at 90%) and the zi
   design's sf and quantiles at its middle quantiles (0.845-0.865). The
   400-replicate study under the old rule (parameters only) had every
   design's distribution parameters a point or two short, these two among
   them; with the point the rule added, that agrees with a real shortfall
   of one to three points at 100 units, which 200 replicates cannot pin
   to particular designs. It is inside the rule and smaller than the
   figures recorded before.

3. More refits do not close it. At 500 refits the edge and zi designs are
   where they were at 200 (0.867 and 0.871 at 90%); the mean over all
   cells moves 0.2 points at 90% and 0.5 at 95%. Paired, the replicates a
   cell covers at one count and not the other are few and split both ways
   (at most 8 of 200 in a cell). The shortfall is not the noise of 200
   draws in the interval's ends; the default n_boot = 200 can stay.

4. The number of failures does not explain it. Within a design, data sets
   with fewer failures than the design's median cover the distribution's
   own parameters a little less (90%: 0.872 of 708 intervals against
   0.896 of 892; 95%: 0.929 against 0.938), but across designs the short
   ones have the most failures (median 71 in lfp_edge, 74 in zi) and the
   ones at their level the fewest (42 and 43). A warning keyed to a small
   number of failures would not flag the data sets that fall short here,
   so none is proposed from this study. A study that varies the sample
   size would be needed to set one.

5. What is left for later. Case resampling was compared already and is no
   better (results_note.txt). Likelihood-ratio bounds that allow for the
   edge (a critical value from the 50:50 mixture of chi-square(0) and
   chi-square(1) for lfp_p and f0) would not touch this shortfall: lfp_p
   and f0 are the cells at or above their level, and on the edge such a
   bound would be less conservative, not wider. The candidates that could
   are a better small-sample interval for the distribution's parameters
   and functions of time under these models: a calibrated (double)
   bootstrap, a studentised interval, or Bayesian credible intervals.
   Each is a larger change with a study of its own.

The calibration test itself is unchanged: no seed, replicate count,
n_boot or tolerance moved.
